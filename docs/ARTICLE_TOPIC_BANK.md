## How to use this bank

1. Pick the highest-priority unwritten brief (Tier 1 first, unless a seasonal window in Tier 2 is open right now).
2. Title = the exact query in the brief, or as close as reads naturally. That is the Crowe discipline: title and slug mirror what the agent types.
3. Check the audience layer on the brief. **[C]** = consumer/newer-agent voice (explain simply). **[I]** = insider voice (lead with the delta, take a position, never explain basics). Per the Writing Standards in `CLAUDE.md`.
4. Verify every number the day you write. Figures below were verified late July 2026 and go stale.
5. Ship per `CONTENT_WORKFLOW.md`: articles.ts + insights.ts, takeaways quotable and stat-dense, cross-link two related pieces, no em dashes.
6. Mark the topic done here (move it to the Published list) so the bank stays current.

**Compliance guardrails (every piece):** no "highest paying" framing, no inducement language, income examples typical not best-case, agent-facing only. See `CONTENT_WORKFLOW.md`.

## What the research says (30-second version)

- **The demand is real and mapped.** Agent search demand concentrates in seven clusters: getting started/certification, money/commissions, FMO/upline, leads/marketing, compliance, selling mechanics, and seasonal. The money, FMO, and compliance clusters skew insider; getting-started and leads skew newer-agent.
- **The Crowe model in one line:** exact-match query titles, year-stamped pages refreshed annually (new URL each year, old years left live), a seasonal publishing calendar, and every article carrying the same plain-dollars offer. We follow the model but win on depth-per-topic and honesty, not page volume.
- **The open lane nobody owns:** money transparency at the mechanics level. Chargeback math, true-up mechanics, commission-statement auditing, first-year cash flow, what to do when a plan goes $0 commission. Competitors either can't say it honestly (conflicted) or stop at definitions.
- **The emotional cluster is FMO/upline.** "50% of Medicare brokers are considering switching uplines this year" (Spark 2026 Agent Needs Report). Releases, book ownership, and contract red flags are the highest-trust, highest-recruiting-value topics we can write. We already lead here; keep extending it.
- **Timeliness windows matter more than volume.** Cert content peaks June-Aug, AEP prep Jul-Sep, first looks/ANOC Sep-Oct, stars early Oct, OEP rules Dec-Jan. Most competitors' compliance content still describes the OLD rules (pre-Oct 1, 2026), which makes stale-SERP displacement a cheap win right now.

### 7. Is selling Medicare still worth it in 2027?
**[I]** · Queries: "is selling medicare worth it", "is being a medicare agent worth it 2027", "medicare agent squeeze"

Candid, numbers-driven, ends on the renewal math. This is also the recruiting piece.

## Title discipline (the Crowe rules, adapted)

- Title mirrors the query. "How to Get Released From a Medicare FMO," not "Thoughts on Releases."
- Year-stamp anything with numbers in it; refresh annually on a fresh slug; leave old years live.
- Front-load the answer: takeaways carry the whole point, stat-dense, quotable (the AI-citation zone).
- One piece, one query cluster. Two phrasings that both matter get two pieces (Crowe's near-duplicate pattern), but only when both have real volume; otherwise we win on depth.
- Skip the gimmicks we documented as not worth copying: the "1"-prefix titles, AHIP answer-sharing framing, thin near-duplicate sprawl.
