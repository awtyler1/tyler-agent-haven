Medicare Advantage vs. Medicare Supplement

The fast, side-by-side way to find the right fit for your client.

Medicare Advantage
Part C · all-in-one private plan
Bundles your hospital, medical, and usually drug coverage into one low-premium plan, with extras, in exchange for using a network.
Often best for
Tighter budgetsMostly local careWants dental / extras
Medicare Supplement
Medigap · fills Original Medicare's gaps
Pairs with Original Medicare and pays the gaps, so you can see any doctor nationwide with very predictable costs, for a higher premium.
Often best for
Doctor freedomTravelersPredictable costsOngoing needs
What to compareMedicare AdvantageMedicare Supplement
Monthly premiumOften $0 (plus Part B)Higher (plus Part B & Part D)

Referrals & prior approvalOften requiredNot required

Prescription drugsUsually built inSeparate Part D plan
Extra benefitsDental, vision, gym, often includedNot included; buy separately
Travel / nationwideBest in your local areaWorks anywhere in the U.S.
Changing laterSwitch yearly at AEPAnytime, may need underwriting
How they actually work
Margaret, 67 · healthy, on a budget

Local in-network doctors, a $0 plan with dental and a gym, small copays as she goes, capped by the yearly max.

Robert, 72 · manages conditions, travels

Any specialist, no referrals. Plan G plus a Part D plan. Higher premium, near-zero when he gets care.

Questions to ask
Keeping specific doctors? Are they in-network?
Do you travel or live elsewhere part of the year?
How is your health, any procedures coming?
Low monthly bill, or few surprises?
How much do dental and extras matter?
