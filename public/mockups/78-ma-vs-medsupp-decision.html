Medicare Advantage or Medicare Supplement?

A simple way to guide your client to the plan that actually fits.

Premium
MA: often $0 · MS: higher
Doctors
MA: network · MS: anyone on Medicare
Costs to use it
MA: copays to a max · MS: very low
Drugs & extras
MA: usually included · MS: separate
Medicare Advantage
Part C · all-in-one private plan
Lean this way if your client…
  • Wants the lowest monthly premium
  • Mostly uses local doctors, fine with a network
  • Wants dental, vision, and extras bundled in
  • Is okay with copays and occasional prior approvals
  • Is generally healthy right now
Medicare Supplement
Medigap · fills Original Medicare's gaps
Lean this way if your client…
  • Wants any doctor or hospital, no networks
  • Travels or lives part of the year elsewhere
  • Wants the most predictable, low out-of-pocket costs
  • Has ongoing health needs or expects care
  • Doesn't mind a higher premium for peace of mind
Remember: pair it with a separate Part D drug plan.
How it works
Margaret, 67 · healthy, on a budget

Local in-network doctors, a $0 plan with dental and a gym, copays as she goes, capped by the yearly max.

How it works
Robert, 72 · manages conditions, travels

Any specialist, no referrals. Plan G plus Part D. Higher premium, near-zero when he gets care.

Questions to ask before deciding
Want to keep specific doctors? In-network?
Travel or live elsewhere part of the year?
How is your health? Procedures coming?
Low monthly bill, or few surprises?
How much do dental and extras matter?
What is comfortable to pay monthly?
