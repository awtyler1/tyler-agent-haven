Why more of your clients are choosing Medicare Advantage

📋What's happening

For years, Original Medicare paired with a Medicare Supplement was called the “Cadillac” of coverage. The market has shifted, and most beneficiaries now drive Medicare Advantage instead.

MA has climbed steadily since the early 2000s. As of 2025, 54% of all Medicare beneficiaries were enrolled in an MA plan (KFF), and 2026 is expected to add roughly a million more.

Supply is keeping up with demand. There are about 3,373 MA plans for 2026, and the average beneficiary can choose from 39 MA plans, 32 of them MAPD.

Why clients like it

  • Bundled coverage. Medical, hospital, and usually drug coverage live in one plan, on one card.
  • Extra benefits most plans include: dental, vision, hearing, plus perks like gym memberships, OTC allowances, and even home-delivered meals.
  • Low premiums. 67% of MAPD members paid $0 in 2026 (KFF).

The trade-offs to be honest about

  • Less predictable costs. Copays and coinsurance add up until they hit the plan’s max out-of-pocket ($9,250 for 2026, often lower).
  • Provider networks. HMO and PPO rules, possible referrals, and their doctor may not be in network.
  • Hard to reverse. Switching back to a Med Supp later can require medical underwriting outside a guaranteed-issue window.
💡Why it matters to you

More of your clients are going to ask for MA, so your value isn’t just enrolling them. It’s matching the right client to the right plan and setting honest expectations.

Do that and you build trust and keep your book. Oversell the $0 premium and gloss over networks, and you get AEP churn, complaints, and lost renewals.

✅What to do now
  1. Start with a needs conversation. Doctors, prescriptions, travel, and budget tell you MA vs. Med Supp before you ever quote.
  2. Always walk through the network and the max out-of-pocket before enrolling. No surprises later.
  3. For healthy clients choosing MA now, flag that switching back to a Med Supp may require underwriting.
  4. Lead with 5-star plans where available. They carry the richest extra benefits.

Sources: KFF and CMS (2025–2026 data).
